CareRide

Reliable scheduled rides to dialysis, chemo and check-ups

Product case study · interactive prototypeCaregiver app ↗Rider app ↗
Problem

Patients on dialysis or chemo need rides 2–3 times a week, at dawn, for months. A cancelled cab means a missed treatment.

Who I designed for

The family member who books for an elderly parent, often from another city, and the parent who rides.

Core promise

An appointment is never missed because of transport.

My role: PM case study

Market research, user and pain-point analysis, RICE prioritization, MVP scope, success metrics, risks, and this prototype.

Prototype: one week of rides

How to use: Sunanda, 68, has dialysis in Pune three times a week; her daughter Neha books her rides from Gurgaon, about 1,400 km away. Click the moments on the left (or Next moment), switch between Neha's and Sunanda's phones above the screen, and read the reasoning for each moment on the right.

    Problem and evidence

    Non-emergency medical travel is frequent, fixed in time and high-stakes, and in India it is mostly handled by family members or whatever cab shows up.

    Pain points for the family booking the ride

    • TopReliability. "Will the car actually come at 6 AM?" A cancellation means a missed dialysis session.
    • TopRecurring scheduling. "She goes Mon, Wed, Fri for six months. I can't book every ride by hand."
    • HighVisibility from afar. "I need to know she got in and reached safely, without calling."
    • MedDriver suitability. "Will the driver help her into the car and be patient?"
    • MedLanguage. "Aai speaks Marathi. Can she talk to the driver if something goes wrong?"

    Why a ride-hailing platform, and what's missing today

    A platform like Uber already has drivers on the road, routing and payments, so it doesn't need to build supply from scratch. Uber Health exists in the US, but there hospitals book rides for patients. In India, the person booking is usually a son or daughter, so this product is designed for the family caregiver, not the hospital.

    Scope and success metrics

    The promise is one sentence: an appointment is never missed because of transport. Everything in v1 serves that promise; the rest waits until it holds.

    In v1

    • Recurring ridesSet Mon/Wed/Fri once for 4–24 weeks; no rebooking every time.
    • Priority drivers with a 10-minute backupOpted-in drivers commit to zero cancellations; if one drops, a backup is assigned within 10 minutes.
    • Night-before confirmationDriver confirms by 9:30 PM, so the family isn't anxious at dawn.
    • Caregiver tracking and alertsPicked up, on the way, reached: Neha knows without calling.
    • Rider simple modeLarge text, two buttons, the rider's own language.

    Next

    • Hospital bookingDialysis centres schedule rides and discharge pickups from their system.
    • Waiting-time pricingPay drivers fairly for waiting through a 4-hour session.
    • Trained CareRide fleetHigher background checks, mobility-assist and first-aid training.
    • Several dependentsOne caregiver managing rides for both parents.

    Not doing

    • Emergency transportAmbulances need medical staff and response times this product can't promise.
    • Medicine delivery or doctor visitsScope creep that dilutes the reliability promise.
    • Medical recordsThe app knows needs (wheelchair, help to the car), never diagnoses.
    North starScheduled-ride completion ≥ 99%

    Rides completed on time ÷ rides scheduled. The product's whole reason to exist.

    RetentionCaregiver rebooking rate

    Families who keep the schedule running the next week.

    GuardrailDriver satisfaction on CareRide trips

    If drivers dislike these trips, supply dries up and reliability breaks.

    Risks and how I'd handle them

    Plan: a 90-day pilot in one city (Pune) before expanding.

    Driver economicsA 4-hour dialysis session means waiting or an empty trip back.Default to a separate return ride in v1; test paid waiting as an opt-in.
    Medical incidents and liabilityStandard ride terms don't cover a patient's health event in the car.Insurance and an emergency protocol (call the family, nearest hospital) before scaling beyond the pilot.
    Driver supply at dawn6 AM pickups are hard to fill reliably.Reliability bonus for priority drivers; release schedules a week ahead so supply can be planned.
    Scope creepAmbulances, medicine delivery and doctor visits are tempting adjacent bets.Explicitly out of v1; revisit only once completion holds at 99%+.
    CompetitionHealth-specific transport startups could move first.Win on reliability and the driver network that already exists; pilot fast.
